📝 본문 (Description)

I spent a daytime visit exploring the Imperial Palace in central Tokyo — walking through the East Gardens inside and along the outer moat that surrounds the grounds.

The East Gardens were peaceful and unhurried — a surprisingly serene space given the city surrounding it. Well-tended greenery and seasonal plants gave the imperial grounds a quiet dignity.

Along the outer perimeter, the moat runs alongside the stone walls — deep and still, it feels like a remnant of the Edo-period fortifications left largely untouched.

It was also striking to see so many locals out running along the outer path — the Imperial Palace perimeter is one of Tokyo's most popular running routes.

The Imperial Palace is the residence of the Japanese Emperor. While the inner palace is closed to the public, the East Gardens (Higashi-Gyoen) are open and free to visit. The outer perimeter, including the moat, stretches about 5km and is one of Tokyo's most popular running routes.
